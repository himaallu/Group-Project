# Published benchmarks reward shortcuts that twins expose

The published literature found for TwinCheck's six datasets uses them for three jobs. MultiFC, AVeriTeC and X-FACT serve text claim verification, M4FC and VERITE serve multimodal fact-checking, and GenImage serves AI-generated image detection. Model families have moved from BiLSTM and mBERT readers (2019–2021) to fine-tuned XLM-R and DeBERTa encoders, and then to GPT-4o and open-weight LLM pipelines (2024–2025). The most useful finding for TwinCheck is that **every one of these datasets has a documented shortcut that inflates headline scores**. Google snippets leak the verdict for **69.7%** of MultiFC misinformation claims. On X-FACT, random snippets score about the same as claim-only models. GPT-4o with no evidence reaches **71.0%** AVeriTeC dev accuracy, against 72.4% for the full system. On VERITE, one image–caption similarity feature, with no evidence at all, reaches **70.89%**, and every CLIP detector stays near chance on miscaptioned pairs. On GenImage, a JPEG-versus-PNG format bias is worth **more than 11 points** of cross-generator accuracy. Within the literature these notes cover, I found no paper on these datasets that swaps an entity inside the evidence to test whether the verdict flips, none that trains a verdict model with a twin-consistency loss, and none that re-encodes true images with a VAE to measure authenticity leakage. TwinCheck can therefore present those three elements as new, with "to our knowledge" wording. The searches were not exhaustive (some papers were read only at abstract level). Scores are rarely comparable across papers, because label spaces, splits and evidence sources differ. TwinCheck's own results appear below only in the clearly marked "Relation to TwinCheck" passages. They come from `reports/project_summary.md` and its `results/` files, not from published work.

## Text benchmarks: MultiFC and AVeriTeC both reward claim-only cues

### MultiFC: 26 label spaces, top-10 Google snippets and heavy verdict leakage

**What it is and how its authors used it.** Augenstein et al. built MultiFC from **34,918 English claims from 26 fact-checking sites**. They kept the sites' **165 original labels** (2–27 per site) and attached the top-10 Google results for each claim ([Augenstein et al., EMNLP 2019](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1475.pdf)). Because the sites share no common label set, they framed the task as multi-task learning with one task per site. A Label Embedding Layer projects all labels into a shared space, and per-site masks limit each prediction to that site's labels. Their best model jointly ranks the snippets and predicts the verdict with BiLSTM encoders, without gold ranking labels. It reached **Micro-F1 0.625 / Macro-F1 0.492** with metadata, against 0.469 / 0.253 for a claim-only model ([Augenstein et al. 2019](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1475.pdf)). The authors flagged two shortcuts themselves. Claim–snippet token overlap drives the evidence rank, and very specific tags co-occur with correct predictions. The public test split carries **dummy labels** ([HF mirror card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/pszemraj/multi_fc)). The release removed only each claim's own fact-check URL, so articles from other fact-checking sites stay in the snippets.

**Later uses fall into four groups.** The first group extends the original per-site task with temporal reasoning. Time-aware evidence ranking raised a BiLSTM from .5521 / .3185 to **.6265 / .3673** Micro/Macro-F1 ([Allein et al., J. Web Semantics 2021](https://researchprofiles.ku.dk/files/284630787/Time_aware_evidence_ranking_for_fact_checking.pdf)). Implicit temporal reasoning with RoBERTa reached **.6882 / .5744**. In the same study, a plain DistilRoBERTa base already scored .6952 / .5532, above the original paper's best Macro-F1 ([Allein et al., Findings EACL 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-eacl.13.pdf)). The second group probes shortcuts. Hansen et al. used only the PolitiFact and Snopes subsets, made their own label-stratified 70/10/20 split, and removed labels that are not about veracity. Their BERT did best on PolitiFact with **evidence only (0.351 / 0.359)**, ahead of claim plus evidence (0.285 / 0.292), so they concluded the models "are inherently not learning to reason" ([Hansen et al., ACL 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07698)). Glockner et al. reused those splits and defined "leaked evidence" as snippets from fact-check articles or snippets that discuss the verdict. They found it for **69.7%** of MultiFC misinformation claims. A BERT model scored **59.7 macro-F1 on leaked PolitiFact claims but 25.6 on unleaked ones**, and a Snopes claim-only model (67.4 F1-micro) beat the full-evidence model (62.7) ([Glockner et al., EMNLP 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.13865)). The third group simplifies the labels for domain transfer. Karisani & Ji binarised the labels ("True" → Support, everything else → Refute) and built five topic domains. A bi-encoder retriever with a RoBERTa reader and adversarial domain adaptation reached **0.618** average macro-F1, against 0.605 for the best baseline and GPT-3 in-context scores of 0.456–0.536 ([Karisani & Ji, NAACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.124.pdf)). The fourth group cites MultiFC critically. One zero- and few-shot benchmark left it out because of noisy evidence ([Pan et al., IJCNLP-AACL 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.09444v1)), and the AVeriTeC authors cite its temporal leaks, where evidence is dated after the claim ([Schlichtkrull et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.13117v3)).

**Relation to TwinCheck (approach).** TwinCheck's design follows the careful follow-up work. It trains on train + validation only, makes its own label-stratified split, and drops labels that are not about veracity (mixture, half-true, misleading, satire), with Hansen et al. as the precedent. It also removes snippets from fact-checking domains, the leak that Glockner et al. measured. I found no published mapping of all 165 labels onto three classes, so `data/label_map.csv` is new as far as these notes show. TwinCheck's 3-class macro-F1 cannot be compared with the per-site averaged Micro/Macro-F1 of Augenstein or Allein, and the report should say so. Scores after cleaning should also be expected to fall below pre-2022 numbers, which leaked evidence inflated. **Relation to TwinCheck (our results, internal).** In our runs on the cleaned MultiFC split (16,847 / 2,104 / 2,106 claims, `results/R1`), a claim-only baseline scored **0.481 macro-F1 but an unrelated rate of 0.01** (`results/F4`). That is the Hansen/Glockner pattern again: good accuracy without reading the evidence. The `_B` and `_C` twins turn that pattern into a direct, per-claim measurement.

### AVeriTeC: clean QA evidence, yet GPT-4o nearly matches itself with no evidence

**What it is and how its authors used it.** AVeriTeC has **4,568 real-world claims from 50 fact-checking organisations**. Its evidence is question–answer pairs backed by web pages dated before the claim, and it has four labels: Supported, Refuted, Not Enough Evidence and Conflicting/Cherrypicking. The splits are temporal (3,068 / 500 / 1,000), Refuted makes up 57–62% of each split, and the licence is CC-BY-NC-4.0 ([Schlichtkrull et al., NeurIPS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13117)). Evidence is scored with Hungarian METEOR over the QA pairs. The headline "AVeriTeC score" counts a verdict as correct only if that evidence score is at least λ = 0.25. The baseline pipeline searched Google, generated questions with BLOOM-7b, reranked and classified stance with BERT-large, and assigned the label by a fixed rule. It scored only **0.15** at λ = 0.25 on dev. With gold evidence, the same classifier reached macro-F1 .49. gpt-3.5-turbo without retrieval got macro-F1 .39 but an AVeriTeC score of only 0.10, because it often invented its evidence ([Schlichtkrull et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13117)).

**Shared tasks and later papers.** At the 2024 FEVER shared task, GPT-4o pipelines led. InFact won with **0.63**, followed by HerO (open Llama models, 0.57), CTU AIC (0.50) and Dunamu-ml (0.50), against a 0.11 baseline ([Schlichtkrull et al., FEVER 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23850)). The winning recipe has four steps: generate questions or queries with an LLM, retrieve with dense embeddings (often after BM25 coarse filtering), answer from the retrieved text, and predict the verdict with a large LLM. HerO's ablation shows that fine-tuning the verdict model matters. A fully fine-tuned Llama-3.1-70b reached **0.752 accuracy / 0.578 AVeriTeC score**, against 0.54 / 0.422 with in-context prompting ([Yoon et al., FEVER 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.fever-1.15/)). CTU AIC reports that a fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-large 4-way classifier reached **0.71 dev macro-F1** on gold evidence ([Ullrich et al., FEVER 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.fever-1.16/)). The 2025 task allowed only open-weight models on one 23 GB GPU, at no more than 60 s per claim, and switched the evidence metric to Ev2R recall. CTU AIC won with Qwen3-14B at **0.3317**, then HerO 2 at 0.2707, against a 0.2023 baseline ([Akhtar et al., FEVER 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.fever-1.15/)). Two classes stay unsolved. In 2025 no system scored above 0.1 on Not Enough Evidence or on Conflicting, and several teams simply stopped their models from predicting those labels.

**Evidence that models do not need the evidence.** InFact's dev ablations are the clearest case. Its GPT-4o "No Evidence" variant reached **71.0%** accuracy, against 72.4% for the full system. With Llama 3, the naive variant with no retrieval (67.0%) beat the full pipeline (61.8%) ([Rothermel et al., FEVER 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.fever-1.12/)). The DEFAME authors report that plain GPT-4o scores 62.1% on AVeriTeC dev but only **35.2%** on ClaimReview2024+, a set of claims made after GPT-4o's training cutoff. They read this as dependence on memorised knowledge ([Braun et al., ICML 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10510)). A non-peer-reviewed October 2026 preprint finds that Qwen3-14B with no evidence beats the always-Refuted baseline on binary AVeriTeC claims (accuracy 0.710 against 0.679), and that masking named entities collapses Supported recall from 0.421 to 0.131 ([Wu Lu et al., arXiv 2610.04888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04888)). One more result is a direct caution for TwinCheck. Adding ChatGPT paraphrases of claims to the stance training data slightly *lowered* gold-evidence macro-F1, from .49 to .46 ([Schlichtkrull et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13117)).

**Relation to TwinCheck (approach).** The labels map cleanly onto TwinCheck's three: Supported → supported, Refuted → contradicted, NEE → unverified. Dropping Conflicting/Cherrypicking (about 6–8% of each split) fits the plan's "drop misleading" rule. TwinCheck's hybrid BM25 + BGE-M3 retrieval followed by a reranker mirrors the hybrid retrieval of HUMANE, UHH and SK_DU, and running Qwen3 in 4-bit is close to the 2025 open-weight regime. The verdict step is where TwinCheck differs. Every winner predicts the verdict with an LLM, while TwinCheck uses per-snippet NLI plus a small MLP head; CTU's DeBERTa result is the nearest precedent for a compact classifier. The paraphrase-augmentation result supports TwinCheck's target of better consistency at no more than 1 point of macro-F1 loss, rather than a promise of higher accuracy. **Relation to TwinCheck (our results, internal).** On our AVeriTeC split (2,309 / 252 / 429), the Qwen3-4B judge scored **0.613** against 0.494 for Qwen3-8B (`results/H7/qwen4b`). That is a 3-class macro-F1, not the official evidence-thresholded AVeriTeC score, so it cannot be set beside the shared-task leaderboard.

## Arabic benchmarks: X-FACT and M4FC are small, skewed and leak through snippets

### X-FACT: multilingual claims where random snippets equal claim-only

**What it is and how its authors used it.** X-FACT has **31,189 non-English claims in 25 languages**. Each claim has 7 labels and the top-5 Google snippets as evidence. Its only Arabic source is misbar.com ([Gupta & Srikumar, ACL 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-short.86.pdf)). It defines three test sets: in-domain α1, out-of-domain α2 and zero-shot α3. Every model is mBERT trained on all languages together. The best, Attn-EA + Meta, reached only **41.9 / 15.4 / 16.0** macro-F1 on α1/α2/α3, against **38.2 / 16.2 / 14.7** for claim-only. Pairing each claim with random snippets from the same language scored 37.5 on α1, so the authors concluded that the dataset "exhibits some claim-only bias". In a hand check of 100 Hindi claims, only 45% of snippets gave enough information to decide the label, against 83% for the full web pages. Adding 12,311 PolitiFact claims from MultiFC lowered every score ([Gupta & Srikumar 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-short.86.pdf)).

**Later uses.** CONCRETE added a cross-lingual retriever over 347,557 BBC passages and raised zero-shot macro-F1 to **19.83**. It also explained why Google evidence helps in-domain but not zero-shot: snippets for the training languages often contain fact-checkers' verdict words. Among Indonesian claims whose results contain "SALAH" (wrong), 95% are labelled partly true or false. Performance also fell as the language of the passage moved further from the language of the claim, Arabic included (Kendall's τ = −0.490) ([Huang et al., COLING 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02071)). Fully fine-tuned XLM-R later reached **57.7 / 47.6 / 43.2**. In the same study, 7–12B LLMs, few-shot or LoRA-tuned, scored only 12.7–16.9 macro-F1. The best of them was a *claim-only* LoRA Qwen 2.5 7B, and the authors note a "systematic difficulty leveraging evidence" ([Shcharbakova et al., FEVER 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20700v1)). A companion paper reports large gaps by writing script, with Latin-script languages doing best ([Shcharbakova et al., RANLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ranlp-1.131/)). The only Arabic-only X-FACT study tested LLMs claim-only on 771 true/false Arabic claims and found cross-lingual prompting best (Llama 3 8B: 59.01 → 86.55 accuracy) ([Gupta et al., AbjadNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.abjadnlp-1.12.pdf)). But **730 of those 771 claims are false**, so always answering "false" would score 94.7%, above every model the paper reports.

### M4FC: multimodal, multilingual claims meant for evaluation only

**What it is and how its authors used it.** M4FC has **4,982 images and 6,980 claims in 10 languages** from 22 IFCN fact-checkers. Arabic is **12.4%** of claims, from fatabyyano.net (658 articles) and verify-sy.com (42 articles). It defines six tasks, including fake-image detection, image contextualisation, location verification and binary verdict prediction ([Geng, Tonglet & Gurevych, arXiv 2510.23508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23508)). The models tested were InternVL2.5-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Llama-3.2-11B-Vision, GPT-4o-mini and Gemini-1.5-Flash, plus the COVE and DEFAME pipelines. In the balanced verdict setting no model passed **80 macro-F1**. Open models lost more than proprietary ones on multilingual claims. Web evidence had the largest single effect on the English verdict, and GPT-4o-mini with gold intermediate outputs plus web evidence reached about 89.2 F1. One off-the-shelf AI-image detector scored **6.41 F1** on real fact-checked images, against 70.31 for a pretrained deepfake detector. Web evidence existed for only 48.62% of images, mostly in English. The authors say the multilingual and balanced settings carry style or language biases and should be used for evaluation only ([Geng et al., v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.23508v2)). The per-language Arabic F1 appears only in a figure. The venue is unconfirmed, and I found no later papers using M4FC.

**Wider Arabic context.** Other Arabic resources are small, or binary, or both. AraFacts has 6,121 naturally occurring claims from five Arabic fact-checking sites ([Sheikh Ali et al., WANLP 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.wanlp-1.26)). On ANS, BERT reached 64.3 F1 on claim verification ([Khouja, FEVER 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.fever-1.2.pdf)). CheckThat! 2025 ran Arabic as True/False only and removed fact-checking sites from the evidence "to avoid leakage". Its top Arabic score of **96.15 macro-F1** comes from that binary task, helped by dense retrieval and fine-tuned LLMs ([Venktesh et al., CLEF 2025](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4038/paper_53.pdf)).

**Relation to TwinCheck (approach).** The Arabic literature points to the same problems TwinCheck targets. Verdict words leak through snippets (CONCRETE), claim-only models score about as well as evidence models (X-FACT), and LLMs struggle to use evidence (Shcharbakova et al.). No X-FACT or M4FC paper I found tests cross-lingual *consistency*, that is, whether the same claim gets the same verdict in English and Arabic. The Arabic data is also extremely skewed (94.7% false in the binary X-FACT subset; true claims are 5.9% of M4FC's imbalanced setting). That supports building balanced Arabic tests as `_A`/`_M` twins of English supported sources rather than relying on native Arabic labels, and reporting macro-F1 instead of accuracy. M4FC's mostly English evidence for claims that are often not English is a natural version of TwinCheck's `_M` twin (Arabic claim, English evidence). Its authors' advice to use the multilingual split for evaluation only fits the plan to use M4FC as a test set only. **Relation to TwinCheck (our results, internal).** Our system without English-only entity features reached Arabic macro-F1 **0.597 against 0.605 in English**, with cross-lingual consistency of 74% (`_A`) and 80% (`_M`). On the 135-claim X-FACT Arabic test set, every system scored at about chance (`results/Y5`). Only 54% of the checked translations were fully correct (`reports/project_summary.md` §6), which limits the `_A` results.

## Image benchmarks: VERITE and GenImage expose similarity and format shortcuts

### VERITE: miscaptioned pairs defeat every similarity-based detector

**What it is and how its authors used it.** VERITE is a test-only set of **1,000 image–caption pairs**: 338 true, 338 miscaptioned (MC) and 324 out-of-context (OOC), from 260 Snopes and 78 Reuters fact-checks ([Papadopoulos et al., IJMIR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14133)). It uses "modality balancing": each image appears once in its true pair and once in an MC pair, and each caption appears once in its true pair and once in an OOC pair. This blocks models that look at only one modality. The authors motivated the design by showing unimodal bias in older benchmarks. An image-only model reached **83.7%** on VMU-Twitter, the best of any model, and a text-only model beat multimodal ones on COSMOS (72.6% against 66.9%) ([VERITE v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.14133v3)). Their CHASMA method creates hard synthetic misaligned training pairs. A DT-Transformer on frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 features, trained on CHASMA-augmented data, reached the best multiclass accuracy of **52.1%**. Results are reported as True-vs-OOC, True-vs-MC and 3-class accuracy.

**Later uses.** RED-DOT added Google web evidence and a relevant-evidence detection module, reaching **76.9** True-vs-OOC ([Papadopoulos et al., arXiv 2311.09939](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09939)). That evidence is badly leaked. EVVER-Net classifies **35.3%** of VERITE's evidence as coming from fact-check articles, and 45.2% for the true and MC classes ([Chrysidis et al., arXiv 2404.18971](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.18971)). The WACV 2025 "Similarity over Factuality" paper is the key shortcut study. AITR reached **82.7** True-vs-OOC, yet only **51.78 True-vs-MC, with MC recall of 13.02%**. The simple MUSE baseline feeds six CLIP similarities to a decision tree, random forest or MLP. Using only the image–caption similarity, with no evidence, MUSE reached **70.89%** on VERITE, and it kept high performance when trained on just 710 samples. The authors' sixth recommendation asks the field for tests showing that models rely on factuality rather than shortcuts ([Papadopoulos et al., WACV 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13488)). LAMAR, trained on synthetic miscaptions generated by VLMs, raised True-vs-MC to **66.0** and multiclass to 54.9 ([arXiv 2504.06010](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06010)). Only retrieval agents that read evidence get past about 80% on the MC split. DEFAME, a zero-shot GPT-4o agent that excludes fact-check domains and pages dated after the claim, scored **78.4 / 83.3 / 83.9** on True-vs-OOC, True-vs-MC and True-vs-False ([Braun et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10510)). MAD-Sherlock (multi-agent GPT-4o debate) reports **85.2** on all of VERITE ([Lakara et al., arXiv 2410.20140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20140)). GPT-4o results on VERITE may be inflated by memorisation, because its fact-checks date from 2001–2023.

**Authenticity and verdicts.** I found no paper that applies AI-generation or VAE perturbations to VERITE images and measures verdict changes. VERITE excludes "asymmetric" cases, such as a manipulated image alone, by design. The nearest result is "Fact or Fake?", on MMFakeBench and DGM4 rather than VERITE. Adding deepfake-detector outputs to fact-checking pipelines **reduced F1 by 0.04–0.08** "due to non-causal authenticity assumptions" ([Sagar et al., arXiv 2602.01854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01854)). TrustLift uses image swaps on VERITE. Hard counterfactual augmentation raised hard-swap detection from 0.402 to 0.737 ([Borodulin et al., ACM IMPACT '26](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3841453.3841505)). MIC-Bench pairs authentic images with AI-generated counterparts, but its AI images deliberately change the content ([arXiv 2609.33441](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33441)).

**Relation to TwinCheck (approach).** VERITE's modality balancing is the closest published precedent for "change exactly one thing". Its MC class, the same image with a wrong date, place or event in the caption, is the image-side analogue of `_B`. Its OOC class is the analogue of `_C`. The difference is that VERITE changes the caption against a fixed image, while TwinCheck changes the evidence against a fixed claim, or, with `_V`, changes the image's AI fingerprint while keeping its content. TwinCheck's MUSE-style "similarity + LightGBM" baseline comes directly from the WACV paper and should get a low ESR, because `_B` twins keep high similarity while changing one fact. One wording caution: CLAUDE.md cites the WACV paper for text word-overlap shortcuts, but its finding is about crossmodal and evidence *similarity* in image–caption checking. The text version is TwinCheck's extrapolation and should be worded that way. Any evidence-based VERITE run should apply the same fact-check-domain filter, given the 35% leakage. **Relation to TwinCheck (our results, internal).** In our leakage test on VERITE, the decoupled verdict head changed **11.1%** of verdicts, against **22.2%** for a fused model that sees the authenticity score, and 25.0% when the fused model also sees the image embedding (`results/F3`). We processed 1,001 pairs (`results/F1`) against the published 1,000. DEFAME's extracted text also says 1,001, so it is worth checking the release before quoting the count.

### GenImage: near-perfect in-domain, near-chance on unseen generators, and a format shortcut

**What it is and how its authors used it.** GenImage pairs **1,331,167 ImageNet real images with 1,350,000 fakes from eight generators** (Midjourney V5, SD V1.4, SD V1.5, ADM, GLIDE, Wukong, VQDM, BigGAN) ([Zhu et al., NeurIPS 2023 D&B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08571)). Its standard protocol trains on SD V1.4 and tests on all eight generators. Every detector scored about 99% in-domain and dropped to near chance on Midjourney, ADM, VQDM and BigGAN. ResNet-50 averaged **72.1%**, DeiT-S 71.6% and Swin-T 74.8%. The authors concluded that CNNs and Transformers perform similarly. In the full 8×8 cross-validation, a ResNet-50 trained on BigGAN scored **49.9%** on SD V1.4, SD V1.5, Midjourney and Wukong. JPEG compression at q=65 dropped plain backbones to about 52%. CNNSpot stayed at 97.3%, but only because it trains with JPEG and blur augmentation ([GenImage](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.08571)).

**Later detectors and the debiasing turn.** UnivFD argued that end-to-end detectors turn "real" into a sink class for anything not fake, and it proposed probing frozen CLIP features instead ([Ojha et al., CVPR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10174)). On GenImage it gave up in-domain accuracy (84.2% on SD V1.4) for better transfer (Midjourney 73.2%, BigGAN 80.3%, mean **73.29**). Hybrid methods went further: GenDet 81.56, PatchCraft 82.30, AIDE **86.88** ([Yan et al., ICLR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19435)). Then "Fake or JPEG?" showed that GenImage's real images are JPEG, mostly at quality 96, with varied sizes, while every fake is a PNG at a fixed size per generator. Compressing the fakes to JPEG 96 and matching sizes raised cross-generator accuracy by **+11.06 points for ResNet-50 and +11.74 for Swin-T** ([Grommelt et al., arXiv 2403.17608](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17608v2), preprint). On B-Free's "GenImage unbiased" test set, many leaderboard methods collapse. NPR fell to 50.1 AUC / 50.7 balanced accuracy and AIDE to 84.2 / 60.2. UnivFD kept 85.4 / 65.5, and B-Free's DINOv2 ViT reached 97.4 / 89.3 ([Guillaro et al., CVPR 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17671)). DDA confirmed the pattern on JPEG-aligned fakes: NPR 51.5, UnivFD 64.1, AIDE 61.2, DDA 91.7 balanced accuracy ([Chen et al., NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14359v6)). Rajan et al. built "aligned" fakes by passing real images only through the latent diffusion autoencoder, so real and fake share content, size and format. A ResNet-50 trained this way was much more robust to resizing and JPEG ([Rajan et al., arXiv 2410.11835](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11835)).

**Relation to TwinCheck (approach).** The plan's frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 logistic probe is the UnivFD recipe. The literature predicts that it will transfer better than end-to-end CNNs, and that ResNet-50 will be the most JPEG-fragile unless trained with JPEG augmentation. The `_V` twin (SD-VAE encode→decode of a true image) is the same construction as Rajan et al.'s aligned fakes and B-Free's self-conditioned reconstructions, and those papers should be cited as its source. Using such reconstructions to measure *verdict* leakage is, to our knowledge, new. The leakage claim is only convincing if the detector actually flags the `_V` twins as AI, so the detector's AI score on `_V` should be reported next to the leakage rate. **Relation to TwinCheck (our results, internal).** We replicated the format shortcut: a model using only file format and size separated real from AI images perfectly (**1.000**), so the detectors were rebuilt on a debiased 96 px input (`results/F3`, `results/F5`). Our protocol reverses GenImage's standard one. We train on BigGAN and test on SD V1.4 as the unseen generator. GenImage's own BigGAN-trained ResNet-50 scored 49.9% on SD V1.4, so our low unseen-generator recall is what the literature predicts, not a pipeline fault. That recall was 0.013 for ResNet-50, 0.126 for ViT-B/16 and 0.156 for the CLIP probe, with AUROC 0.610 / 0.754 / 0.620 (`reports/project_summary.md` §5).

## Where TwinCheck fits: every benchmark has a shortcut, none has a twin test

The table below sets out, for each dataset, its main published use, the shortcut the literature documents, and the gap TwinCheck targets. The "TwinCheck" column describes our approach, not published work.

| Dataset | Main published use and models | Documented shortcut | TwinCheck use and gap targeted |
|---|---|---|---|
| MultiFC | Per-site veracity (BiLSTM → DistilRoBERTa); PolitiFact/Snopes shortcut probes (BERT); binarised domain transfer | Leaked evidence in 69.7% of misinformation claims; claim-only ≈ or > evidence | 3-way main train/test set; fact-check filtering; `_B`/`_C`/`_P` twins measure per-claim evidence use |
| AVeriTeC | QA-evidence verification; LLM RAG pipelines (GPT-4o, Llama-70B, Qwen3-14B) | GPT-4o with no evidence ≈ full system; memorisation; NEE and Conflicting unsolved | Clean English test set; small NLI + MLP head instead of an LLM verdict |
| X-FACT | Multilingual veracity (mBERT → XLM-R; 7–12B LLMs) | Random snippets ≈ claim-only; verdict words in Google snippets | Arabic NLI training and Arabic test; cross-lingual consistency untested in prior work |
| M4FC | Six multimodal tasks; VLMs and DEFAME | Style/language bias in the multilingual split (authors' warning) | Arabic test only; natural Arabic-claim/English-evidence setting like `_M` |
| VERITE | Image–caption OOC/MC detection (CLIP transformers → GPT-4o agents) | Image–caption similarity alone gives 70.89%; MC near chance; 35% leaked evidence | Image-authenticity leakage test with content-preserving `_V` twins |
| GenImage | Cross-generator AI-image detection (CNN, ViT, CLIP probes) | JPEG/PNG and size bias worth more than 11 points | CNN vs ViT vs CLIP probe on debiased input; detector kept out of the verdict |

Two patterns stand out. First, the field has converged on the same protections TwinCheck builds into `clean.py`. AVeriTeC limits evidence to pages before the claim date, DEFAME drops fact-check domains and post-claim pages, and CheckThat! 2025 drops fact-check sites "to avoid leakage". TwinCheck's cleaning rules are standard practice rather than idiosyncratic. Second, the nearest published relatives of twins all change the *input claim or image*: VERITE's balanced pairs, CHASMA and LAMAR synthetic miscaptions, and TrustLift's image swaps. Training with paraphrases, the one published form of invariance training on these datasets, slightly hurt AVeriTeC. TwinCheck holds the claim fixed, perturbs the *evidence*, and adds a KL consistency term plus sensitivity targets to the loss. In the notes reviewed, I found no counterpart to that loss on any of the six datasets. These absences rest on searches that were targeted, not complete (for example, no full citation sweep of VERITE's 110+ citing papers). Some sources are preprints or have unconfirmed venues (M4FC, LAMAR, "Fake or JPEG?", the October 2026 "No Hindsight" paper), so the report should mark them as such.

## Conclusion

The literature changes how TwinCheck's headline result should be framed. Our finding that accuracy alone would have picked the model that reads the evidence least (claim-only and fine-tuned Qwen3-4B lead on macro-F1 but trail on ESR and the unrelated rate, `results/F4`, `results/H7/qwen4b`) is not an oddity of our setup. It is the expected outcome on benchmarks whose evidence leaks the verdict (MultiFC, X-FACT, VERITE), whose claims LLMs have memorised (AVeriTeC), or whose classes differ in file format (GenImage). Each published shortcut study measures the problem *in aggregate*: leaked versus unleaked subsets, no-evidence ablations, similarity-only features, debiased test sets. Twins measure it *per claim* and can be used as a training signal. That is the cleanest way to state TwinCheck's contribution against this body of work.

The literature also sets a bar our twins do not yet meet. VERITE's miscaptions are human-written from real fact-checks, while our automatic `_B` swaps were judged valid in only 22.5% of hand-checked cases (`reports/project_summary.md` §5). Our ESR therefore measures sensitivity to corrupted evidence more than to genuine contradiction. Pairing each twin metric with its validity rate, and naming human-curated swaps as the next step, would make the comparison with VERITE-style benchmarks honest rather than flattering.
