# H7 notes (Himasri, Thu 19 Nov) — model selection, after the professor asked why we chose each model

- Verdict head: trees and logistic regression were tried on the same features. LightGBM wins macro-F1 without twins (38.4 vs MLP 36.3) but is brittle (paraphrase consistency 66.7, unrelated 7.4). Twins as extra data help every model (ESR ~80), but the twin loss on the MLP adds +9 ESR points (p < 0.001) and keeps paraphrase consistency at 91; the KL "keep the answer" term needs a differentiable model, which is the main reason for the MLP.
- NLI: mDeBERTa-v3-base is the smallest and fastest multilingual option; XLM-R-large (+1.5 EN, +6 AR) and English DeBERTa-v3-large (+6 EN, no Arabic) score higher but within overlapping CIs on 150 pairs per language, at 2x the size and time. Possible upgrade if time allows: XLM-R-large features for the verdict head.
- Context for the talk: the claim-only baseline (no evidence) has the highest macro-F1 (48.1, `results/F4/audit_E0.csv`), so MultiFC accuracy alone rewards the shortcut; only the twin-loss model also passes the evidence tests (ESR 89.7, unrelated 100).
- Quantized Qwen3-4B (professor's suggestion) vs Qwen3-8B: claim extraction is equal (F1 0.977 vs 0.984), explanation faithfulness is slightly higher (0.352 vs 0.320), and as an LLM judge 4B is much better (MultiFC macro-F1 0.391 vs 0.233, AVeriTeC 0.613 vs 0.494, both p < 0.001) at half the size. The two judges fail in opposite directions: 8B rarely says contradicted (recall 0.06, so it looks good on unrelated evidence by answering unverified), 4B rarely says unverified (recall 0.01, unrelated rate 0.14). Neither passes all twin tests the way the twin-loss head does (ESR 0.90, unrelated 1.00).
